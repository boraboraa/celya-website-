The models

The four ways to get your phone answered

When a business number rings there are only four outcomes: someone in the house picks up, an outside provider picks up, a machine records, or nobody answers. Those four outcomes do not cost the same — but above all they do not have the same cost structure, and it is the structure that decides what happens when your business changes. A model that holds at ten calls a day can turn absurd at sixty, and the reverse is true as well.

Frequently asked questions

Phone answering service pricing: your questions

What happens if I go over my minutes?
You move up to the plan above, and nothing else changes. We never charge by the minute: an overage billed on the meter turns a good month — plenty of calls, so plenty of customers — into a nasty surprise, and that is exactly the opposite of what we want to sell you. If it is a one-off, we leave it alone.
How do I know which plan to take?
By your minutes, not by your turnover. Take the average length of a call at your place, multiply it by the number of calls you get in a month, and you land on the plan. If you are torn between two, take the lower one: moving up happens on its own, moving down means cancelling.
Does the price depend on the hours covered?
No, and that is the real structural difference with a human answering service. We pay no night-shift wage: covering a Saturday costs us no more than a Tuesday. That is what puts 24-hour cover within reach of a self-employed person, which a classic phone answering service cannot do.
Is there a setup fee?
Not as a rule. There is real configuration work — your hours, your services, your transfer rules, your standard answers — and that is what separates an agent that answers properly from a generic one. When that work is charged, it is capped at one month of your plan, never more.
Do I have to commit for a fixed term?
The commitment terms are part of the proposal and are open to discussion. What we ask above all is that you call the number and hear the service: nobody should sign for a voice they have never heard.
Do I pay per call or a flat fee?
A flat fee, and only a flat fee. You pay a monthly amount that includes a volume of minutes; beyond that, you change plan. There is no price per call, no price per minute, and no meter running while you read this page.
Is it cheaper than a human answering service?
We do not compare figures we have not verified ourselves, and we name no competitor. What this page compares is the models: who pays for what, and why the price follows the hours in one case and not in the other.
What if my call volume changes?
A heating engineer triples his volume in November, an accountancy firm in February. That is expected, and it is one of the reasons the proposal comes after looking at your year, not just your week.
Can I stop?
You switch off call forwarding at your operator, and your calls come back to you as before. You have installed nothing, migrated nothing and changed no number: so there is nothing to dismantle.
Where is the data hosted?
Our clients' calls are processed and their data hosted in the European Union, in line with the GDPR. You can ask for it to be deleted at any time.
Why does nobody leave a message on voicemail?
Voicemail's problem is not its price, it is whether it works. A caller with an immediate need — a leak, a door slammed shut, an appointment to fix — has no reason to talk to a machine that will not answer them. They hang up before the beep and ring the next name on the list. Voicemail does not lose the call: it turns it into silence, which is worse, because you believe you have a solution. The recorded message keeps its uses — announcing a closure, giving an on-call number, pointing people elsewhere — and we have gathered twenty-five of them, ready to copy, on a page of their own. But a message is not a welcome.
Why does a human service cost more evenings and weekends?
Because behind every hour covered there is a wage. The principle of the human phone answering service is an old one and it works: you forward your calls to a call floor, and a real person answers in your company's name. But the price follows the opening hours mechanically: cover in office hours is affordable, cover in the evening and at weekends costs a good deal more. That is why most self-employed people have never had 24-hour cover — not because they did not want it, but because the model put it out of reach at their scale.
What does a voice agent change in the sums?
Not the nature of the service: it holds a conversation, books an appointment, takes a message, transfers on rules. What it changes is the structure of the cost. The price does not follow the opening hours, several calls are handled at the same time, there is no installation and no hardware, you keep your number and your operator, and a seasonal peak calls for no hiring. That, and nothing else, is what puts continuous cover within reach of a small business.
What does a lost call cost me?
Do the exercise on your own business, with your own figures. How many calls do you miss in a week? What share of them would have turned into a job or an order? What is an average job worth at your place? Multiply. Whatever the result, it can be set against every model in the table. We deliberately put no figure here: the missed-call statistics doing the rounds online are almost all without a verifiable source, and we are not going to add one. Your own reading beats any average.
