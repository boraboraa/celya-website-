The phone always rings at the worst moment

Published on 13 August 20266 min readField notes

What we heard talking with Belgian restaurant owners — and why phone reservations remain a sore point.

The moment the phone rings

In a restaurant, the phone never rings when you’re available. It rings at half past twelve. It rings at a quarter to eight. It rings exactly when the dining room is full, when the kitchen is sending out plates, when everyone is on the move.

That’s the paradox of the restaurant business: the hours with the most incoming calls are precisely the hours when nobody can pick up.

What we found

Before building anything for restaurants, we talked with restaurant owners. Here’s what comes up, almost every time.

Nobody’s job is the phone

In the vast majority of establishments, there is no one whose job is to answer. It’s the owner, a waiter between two tables, sometimes someone in the kitchen wiping their hands. The phone is everyone’s task, so it’s no one’s.

Answering mid-service means abandoning your table

A waiter who picks up is a waiter who’s no longer on the floor. He scribbles a reservation in a notebook, maybe gets the time wrong, and goes back to work. Meanwhile, two customers are waiting for the bill.

The cost of the call isn’t the call: it’s the interruption of service.

Calls pile up at the same moment

It’s never one isolated call. At peak time, the phone rings while you’re already on the phone. The second caller hits a busy line — and books somewhere else.

Reservations taken “on the side” create double bookings

One reservation at the counter, another by text message, a third on a scrap of paper. Without one central system, the risk of overselling the room is permanent — and an oversold table on a Saturday night costs you dearly.

Large tables deserve a real decision

A table of two, you take without thinking. A table of twelve on a Friday night is a decision: it commits the room, the kitchen, the service. That kind of request should never be handled in a rush between two dishes.

What it costs

In the restaurant business, a lost reservation isn’t just one cover lost — it’s often a whole table, and sometimes a customer who never comes back.

What we built

That’s where our phone assistant started. The goal was never to “replace someone” — in most restaurants, there’s no one to replace. It was to make sure the phone stops competing with the service.

She picks up every call, even during the rush

French, Dutch, English: she switches automatically to the caller’s language. No customer hits a busy line, none hangs up for lack of an answer.

A capacity plan, not a simple diary

We took inspiration from restaurant booking software — but plugged in where none of them goes: the phone.

Zenchef, Resengo, TheFork handle your online bookings very well. It’s their trade, and they do it well: Resengo alone equips more than 3,500 restaurants in Belgium. But the phone keeps ringing into the void. None of those tools picks up.

That’s exactly the gap we fill. And our capacity plan thinks in covers, not in time slots:

  • It knows the real capacity of your dining room.
  • It knows a table of 2 doesn’t stay as long as a table of 6 — the duration adapts to the party size.
  • It manages the services (lunch, dinner) separately.
  • It applies a safety margin and a minimum notice period if you want one.

The capacity plan, at a glance

You see, service by service, how many covers are already booked and how many remain available. Every reservation taken lowers the counter in real time.

Reservations taken outside the system stay in the picture

A customer books at the counter? A regular sends you a message? You block the seats manually — and the assistant takes it into account immediately. She will never promise a table that no longer exists.

That’s what prevents overbooking, and it’s also why you can trust her.

Large tables follow your rule

Beyond a number of covers you define — ten, twelve, whatever you want — the request doesn’t become an automatic booking. Two options, your choice:

  • she takes down the request and you call back to confirm;
  • or she transfers the call straight to you, so you decide yourself.

The decisions that commit your dining room remain yours.

Every call is sorted and filed

Not every call is a reservation. A supplier, a job application, a question about allergens, a customer who wants to change their time: the assistant identifies the reason and files the call. You see at a glance what deserves a callback and what’s already handled.

The transcript, to go back in time

A doubt about what was said? Every call is transcribed and summarised. You reread the exact conversation, calmly, after service.

Two ways to plug in the service

  • On your current number: you change nothing, you simply forward your calls whenever you want.
  • Or with a dedicated number reserved for bookings.

What if you already use booking software?

You keep it. Your online tool keeps working exactly as before: it receives the web bookings, as it does today.

Celya only takes care of the phone channel — the one those tools don’t cover. The two coexist without stepping on each other, and you simply block in the capacity plan whatever was booked through the other channel.

What it really changes

Service is no longer interrupted by the ringtone. Reservations come in on their own, consistent with the room’s real capacity. Large tables go through you. And in the evening, everything is written down in black and white.

You cook and you serve. The phone is handled.
